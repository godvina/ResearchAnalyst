1 Procurement Collusion

Bid-rigging, complementary bidding, bid rotation, and bid suppression in government procurement. The most common antitrust violation prosecuted by DOJ.

Elements of Offense

  • Agreement or conspiracy between parties
  • Bid-rigging or complementary bidding
  • Market allocation among conspirators
  • Price-fixing or bid suppression
  • Interstate commerce nexus
  • Damages to government or taxpayers

Applicable Statutes

  • Sherman Act Section 1 (15 U.S.C. § 1)
  • 18 U.S.C. § 1341 (Mail Fraud)
  • 18 U.S.C. § 1343 (Wire Fraud)
  • 18 U.S.C. § 371 (Conspiracy to Defraud US)

Detection Patterns

  • Bid rotation: Consistent winner/loser pairs across jurisdictions
  • Complementary bids: Identical percentage spreads
  • Geographic exclusivity: Vendors never compete in adjacent regions
  • Subcontract reciprocity: Loser always gets subcontract from winner

Priority OSINT Sources

🏛️SAM.govVendor registrations, exclusions
📄FPDS.govFederal contract awards
🚚State DOT BidsHighway/construction tabulations
💰USASpendingFederal spending data

2 Price Fixing

Horizontal agreements to fix, raise, or stabilize prices among competitors. A per se violation — no rule of reason defense available.

Elements of Offense

  • Agreement among competitors
  • Fixing or stabilizing prices
  • Horizontal relationship (not vertical)
  • Interstate commerce nexus
  • Per se violation applicability

Applicable Statutes

  • Sherman Act Section 1 (15 U.S.C. § 1)
  • Per se rule — horizontal price-fixing

No need to prove market power or anticompetitive effect. The agreement itself is the violation.

Detection Patterns

  • Correlated bid spreads: Statistical correlation across unrelated contracts
  • Synchronized increases: Competitors raise prices in lockstep
  • Identical markups: Same percentage margins across firms
  • Price parallelism: Movement correlation exceeds market norms

Priority OSINT Sources

📄FPDS.govContract pricing history
💰USASpendingAward obligation trends
🚚State DOT BidsBid amount patterns
📰News/PressIndustry price announcements

3 Market Allocation

Agreements to divide markets by geography, customer, or product line. Another per se violation — competitors carve up territory instead of competing.

Elements of Offense

  • Agreement among competitors
  • Geographic or customer division
  • Horizontal relationship
  • Interstate commerce nexus
  • Per se violation applicability

Applicable Statutes

  • Sherman Act Section 1 (15 U.S.C. § 1)
  • Per se rule — horizontal market allocation

Geographic division, customer allocation, and product-line splitting are all per se illegal when between competitors.

Detection Patterns

  • Geographic exclusivity: Vendors win only in specific regions, never competing in adjacent areas
  • Customer segmentation: Firms divide customers by type or size
  • Product line division: Competitors each take specific categories
  • Territorial boundaries: Align suspiciously with competitor presence

Priority OSINT Sources

🏛️SAM.govNAICS codes, geographic presence
📄FPDS.govPlace of performance patterns
🏢State RegistriesCorporate filings, addresses
🚚Bid TabulationsGeographic bid distribution

4 Merger Review

Potentially anticompetitive mergers or acquisitions that may substantially lessen competition. Evaluated under Clayton Act Section 7 and Hart-Scott-Rodino framework.

Elements of Offense

  • Substantially lessen competition
  • Relevant market definition
  • Market concentration increase
  • Barriers to entry
  • Elimination of competition
  • HHI threshold exceeded

Applicable Statutes

  • Clayton Act Section 7 (15 U.S.C. § 18)
  • Hart-Scott-Rodino Act (15 U.S.C. § 18a)
  • FTC Act Section 5 (15 U.S.C. § 45)

Detection Patterns

  • HHI thresholds: Pre/post-merger concentration exceeding DOJ guidelines
  • Maverick elimination: Acquiring a disruptive competitor
  • Vertical foreclosure: Integration creating supply bottlenecks
  • Serial acquisition: Roll-up strategy in concentrated markets

Priority OSINT Sources

📈SEC EDGAR10-K, 8-K, proxy statements
🏢State RegistriesCorporate structure, officers
📰News/PressM&A announcements
⚖️PACERPrior antitrust challenges

5 Monopolization

Exclusionary conduct by a dominant firm to willfully acquire or maintain monopoly power. Analyzed under rule of reason — requires proving both power and anticompetitive conduct.

Elements of Offense

  • Monopoly power in relevant market
  • Willful acquisition or maintenance of power
  • Anticompetitive conduct (exclusionary acts)
  • Relevant market definition
  • Harm to competition (not just competitors)

Applicable Statutes

  • Sherman Act Section 2 (15 U.S.C. § 2)
  • Rule of reason analysis

Must prove both monopoly power (typically >70% market share) AND exclusionary conduct. Mere size is not illegal.

Detection Patterns

  • Market dominance: Share above 70% in defined relevant market
  • Exclusive dealing: Contracts foreclosing rival access
  • Predatory pricing: Below-cost pricing to eliminate competitors
  • Tying arrangements: Leveraging dominance across markets
  • Refusal to deal: Cutting off competitors after prior dealing

Priority OSINT Sources

📈SEC EDGARMarket share, revenue data
📄FPDS.govSole-source contracts
⚖️PACERPrior antitrust cases
📰News/PressCompetitor complaints

6 Criminal Cartel

Criminal conspiracy involving hardcore cartel conduct, often international. Carries prison sentences up to 10 years and fines up to $100M for corporations.

Elements of Offense

  • Agreement or conspiracy
  • Per se illegal conduct (hardcore cartel)
  • Knowing and willful participation
  • Interstate commerce nexus
  • Identifiable victims
  • Quantifiable damages

Applicable Statutes

  • Sherman Act Section 1 — criminal penalty
  • 18 U.S.C. § 1341 (Mail Fraud)
  • 18 U.S.C. § 1343 (Wire Fraud)
  • Federal Sentencing Guidelines § 2R1.1

Detection Patterns

  • International coordination: Cross-border schemes spanning multiple countries
  • Long-running conspiracy: Multi-year schemes with many participants
  • Leniency indicators: One party cooperating with authorities
  • Parallel conduct: Coordinated behavior across multiple markets
  • Communication patterns: Meetings, calls suggesting coordination

Priority OSINT Sources

⚖️PACERCriminal antitrust cases
📰News/PressInternational enforcement
📈SEC EDGARDisclosure of investigations
🏛️SAM.govExclusion/debarment records
